📞 Emergency Info

Hopefully never needed — good to have anyway

Emergency numbers

999 (or 112)

Police, ambulance, or fire — a crime in progress or genuine emergency. Both numbers work anywhere in the UK.

101

Non-emergency police — to report a crime that already happened, or general police enquiries.

111

NHS non-emergency medical advice — if you're unwell but it's not a 999 situation.

📍 Directions

UK driving tips (this one's for the rental)

Drive on the left

Obvious, but the muscle memory takes a day or so. Roundabouts go clockwise — give way to traffic already on the roundabout, coming from the right.

Speed is in mph

Not km/h — UK road signs use miles. Motorway limit is typically 70mph, single carriageways often 60mph, built-up areas 20-30mph.

Fuel is sold by the litre

Rental cars are very likely manual/stick-shift unless specifically booked as automatic — worth double-checking your reservation now if that matters.

Single-track roads (Scotland especially)

Common in the Highlands. Use passing places to let oncoming cars through, and to let faster traffic overtake.

Congestion Charge & ULEZ

If driving into central London (unlikely on this itinerary since Day 1-2 are on foot), be aware of daily charges for driving in the zone.

Useful apps

Google Maps

Download offline maps for the Highlands before you go — signal gets patchy around Kirkmichael and the Cairngorms.

Citymapper

Better than Google Maps specifically for London public transit (Tube, buses, DLR for Greenwich).

Trainline

If any train legs come up, this is the standard UK train-booking app.

NHS App

Useful if anyone needs to find a pharmacy or urgent care while traveling.

Met Office Weather

More accurate for UK/Scotland weather than most default weather apps — worth it for checking Cairngorms conditions before Day 8.
